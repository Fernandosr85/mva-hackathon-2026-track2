## 0:00 – 0:30 · The unresolved allele

> **[Visual: the two variants, side by side]**
> `c.2210T>G p.Leu737Ter` — nonsense
> `c.3006T>G p.Asn1002Lys` — missense, **VUS**

**Spoken:**

"Track 1 found the BUB1B pair. The nonsense allele is straightforward. The other is a
missense variant, still classified as uncertain.

Here is what nobody has determined. A missense variant can break BubR1 two ways. It can
make a protein that does not work. Or it can make a protein that works fine but does not
survive — it gets degraded, so the cell never has enough.

Those are different diseases at the molecular level, and they call for opposite
therapies. For this variant, nobody has checked which one it is."

> **[Visual: two branches — DEFECTIVE vs SCARCE]**

## 0:30 – 1:25 · The proteasome paradox

> **[Visual: one arrow splitting into two opposite arrows]**

**Spoken:**

"Suppose it is the second one — a working protein that gets degraded.

Then stop the degradation. Block the proteasome, the protein accumulates, the checkpoint
might recover. The original BUB1B study showed a proteasome inhibitor preventing exactly
that clearance.

But a second effect runs the other way. Aneuploid cells depend more heavily on protein
degradation than normal cells — shown in engineered models, hundreds of cancer lines, and
in how myeloma patients respond to proteasome inhibitors. Block the proteasome and
aneuploid cells die.

So the same drug class has two predicted effects here. Rescue upstream. Kill downstream.

And counting live cells cannot tell them apart. Fewer cells might be selective killing,
or plain toxicity. More cells might be rescue, or nothing happening at all."

> **[Visual: RESCUE ← proteasome inhibition → KILL]**

## 1:25 – 2:05 · The HSP90 veto

> **[Visual: aneuploidy → 17-AAG, then a red line back to BubR1]**

**Spoken:**

"Here is why mechanism has to come first.

Look only at the downstream phenotype — aneuploidy — and the literature points you at
HSP90 inhibition. It is a validated aneuploidy-selective vulnerability.

But HSP90 is a chaperone. In these BubR1 mutants, HSP90 is precisely what folds the
unstable protein and keeps it from being degraded. Inhibit it here and you attack the
downstream consequence while destroying what is left of the upstream protein.

**A drug selected from the downstream phenotype can worsen the upstream genetic
lesion.**

A screen returns that compound as a hit, with nothing to flag the conflict."

---

## 2:05 – 3:00 · The decisive experiment

> **[Visual: three boxes, gated left to right]**

**Spoken:**

"So the proposal is the experiment that decides which strategy survives.

**First**, classify the variant. How much protein, how fast it turns over — and
critically, whether it works when you equalise the amount. That separates broken from scarce.

**Second**, perturb the proteasome measuring rescue and killing at once — protein levels,
checkpoint function, missegregation, stress, viability, same cells.

**Third**, test selectivity against matched controls. In MVA, aneuploid cells also exist
outside the tumour.

And the falsifier, registered in advance. If the variant is stable and working normally,
it is not the second hit — and this proposal loses its premise.

The experiment can destroy the idea it was built to test. That is the point."

## Delivery notes

**Pace.** 430 spoken words: 2:46 at 155 wpm, 2:52 at 150, **2:58 at 145**. At 145 the
script fits, but with essentially no pause margin — about two seconds. **Time a natural
read-through before recording**, with the HSP90 pause, the slide changes and the closing
line included. That measurement, not this arithmetic, is the one that decides.

- **≤ 2:55** — record.
- **2:56–3:00** — cut 10 to 15 more words from block 4.
- **> 3:00** — cut 20 to 25, still from block 4.

Never from the paradox or the HSP90 veto. Those two are the pitch.

Two earlier drafts of this file stated word counts that had not been recounted after
editing, and a third reported 2:57 at 145 wpm by truncating 177.9 seconds instead of
rounding it. Every figure above was recomputed.


**The one line to land.** *"A drug selected from the downstream phenotype can worsen the
upstream genetic lesion."* Pause before it and after it. It is the whole argument for
mechanism-first repurposing, and it is the sentence a judge will remember.

**Say "the child", not "the patient".** This is one specific kid whose family released
their data.

**Do not say** "we propose treating MVA with bortezomib." Say "an approved drug class
that merits genotype-conditioned investigation in patient cells." The distinction is
the proposal's credibility.

**Name no drug in the first two minutes.** The mechanism has to arrive first, or the
video becomes a drug pitch with mechanism attached — which is exactly the ordering the
proposal argues against.

**End on the falsifier, not on the hypothesis.** Every other pitch ends on why their
idea might work. Ending on what would kill it is the differentiator, and it takes eight
seconds.

## What the script forces back into Draft 0.3

Writing this exposed three things the report still gets wrong:

1. **The title buries the lede.** "A Genotype-Conditioned Proteostasis Strategy" is
   accurate and forgettable. The paradox is the finding; the title should carry it.
2. **The executive summary opens with Track 1.** The video opens with the unresolved
   allele, which is the actual entry point. The summary should too.
3. **The HSP90 veto is section 5.3.** In the video it is a third of the runtime, because
   it is the most original thing in the work. In the report it is buried in the middle
   of a landscape survey.

If the video is the compression test, the report failed it in those three places.
